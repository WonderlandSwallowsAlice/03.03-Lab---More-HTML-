Gubkin, Belgorod Oblast, Russia

Location

Gubkin is a town in Belgorod Oblast, Russia, located 138 kilometers (86 mi) northeast of Belgorod on the Oskolets River, a tributary of the Oskol River.

About

History

Gubkin is a city in Belgorod Oblast, Russia, known for its significant role in the iron ore industry. It was founded in 1939 and named in honor of Ivan Gubkin, who played a crucial role in the discovery and development of the Kursk Magnetic Anomaly, a vast iron ore deposit. The city's economy is heavily reliant on mining and metallurgy, with the Lebedinsky Mining and Processing Plant being a major employer. Gubkin is also home to the Gubkin State Mining and Technical University, which trains specialists for the mining industry. The city offers several cultural attractions, including the Museum of Local Lore and the Palace of Culture, providing insights into the region's history and industrial heritage. (Source: Wisdom Library )

Population

Gubkin's population is 83,766 people.

Lebedinsky Mine (GOK)

The Lebedinskiy mine, officially known as Lebedinsky GOK, is the largest open-pit iron ore mining and beneficiation facility in Russia and the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS), situated in Gubkin, Belgorod Oblast, near the Lebedinsky iron ore deposit. Operated by Metalloinvest MC LLC, it exploits proven reserves of 3.8 billion tonnes of iron ore through open-pit methods and produces high-quality outputs including iron ore concentrate, non-fluxed and fluxed pellets, and hot-briquetted iron (HBI), with the latter making it the sole HBI producer in Russia and the CIS. Established as a cornerstone of Russia's ferrous metallurgy sector, the mine supports global steel production with its efficient processing technologies and substantial output capacity.
